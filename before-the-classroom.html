Before the classroom

Class shapes who has seen computer science before Intro CS

Long before a student can sign up for Intro or AP Computer Science, family income shapes how much computing they have seen at home and in the programs around school. A 2015 Google and Gallup survey found lower-income students had the least access to computer science learning at school. By high school, that gap shows up in who is sitting in the class.

The technical version

Both bars in the first chart come from one sentence in Education Week, which reports Code.org's 2024 State of CS data. The gap is the difference between the two published percentages. The second chart stays empty on purpose. In 2015, Google and Gallup surveyed 9,693 K-12 principals at every grade level, and three in four said their school did not offer computer science with programming or coding. In 2025, Code.org reports that 60% of U.S. public high schools offer foundational CS, counted from state course data. The two figures cover different schools (all K-12 grades vs. public high schools), define CS differently (with programming vs. foundational CS) and come from different methods (a survey vs. course records), so putting them side by side would suggest a change the data can't show.

What happened at elite colleges after the 2023 ruling

In June 2023 the Supreme Court ended race-conscious admissions in SFFA v. Harvard. The class that started college in fall 2023 was the last one admitted under the old rules. Comparing it with fall 2025 shows what the first two classes admitted under the ruling look like at the most selective colleges.

The technical version

Bloomberg and Higher Ed Today count different sets of colleges (27 and 29) and don't name them all, so their numbers are shown side by side, not combined. Bloomberg measures Black students as a share of the first-year class but says only "enrollment" for Hispanic students. Higher Ed Today doesn't say which year it compares fall 2025 with. The before-and-after averages come from one dataset instead: James Murphy's tracker of 29 elite colleges. Each value is a simple average of the colleges' published shares, so a small college counts as much as a large one. Murphy's "before" is the average of the 2022 and 2023 classes. Nine colleges count multiracial students in more than one group; leaving them out gives the same direction of change.

The national research view. EdWorkingPaper 26-1392 (Bloem et al., 2026) links 12.6 million domestic PSAT, SAT and AP takers in the 2021 to 2024 high school graduation cohorts to National Student Clearinghouse enrollment records. It sorts more than 2,600 colleges into tiers by pre-pandemic acceptance rate and compares fall 2024, the first class admitted after SFFA, with earlier years. Its two headline findings are below.

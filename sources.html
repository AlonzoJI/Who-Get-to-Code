Methods and sources

How the numbers on this site were gathered and checked

Every figure on this site comes from a named source, with a date and a link. Before a number was marked verified, its wording was checked against the source itself, and the exact sentence was saved in the data files under /data. Anything not yet checked is shown with a placeholder badge.

How the datasets fit together

Figures are shown as each source reported them. The site does not merge datasets into new statistics, with two exceptions. Some pages show simple arithmetic on published numbers, such as the 15-point gap between 53% and 38%, and the note always says so. The pipeline diagram on the overview page will multiply each stage's rate once those rates are verified; until then it removes no one.

The six pipeline stages draw on different datasets: Code.org and the Civil Rights Data Collection for access and enrollment, College Board for AP exams, IPEDS for college enrollment and degrees, NSF NCSES and the American Community Survey for jobs and earnings.

What the data can't tell you

  • Different groups of students. Each dataset follows its own cohort and year, so the pipeline describes a typical path, not one class of students followed for ten years.
  • Different definitions. Sources count shares in some places and raw enrollment in others, use "students" or "high school students," and draw their own lines around "elite" or "highly selective" colleges. Notes on each figure say which.
  • Secondhand reporting. Several figures come from news coverage of a report rather than the report itself. The figure table says which source was read.
  • Too soon to tell. Most outcome data covers only the first one or two classes after 2023, and the newest federal data lags by a year or more.
A data problem

The end of corporate diversity reports limits what can be tracked

Google, Meta and Microsoft did not publish diversity data in 2025. Their reports were among the few public records of who gets hired in tech and at what level. Without them, stage 06 of the pipeline depends on federal surveys, which don't report company by company and come out more slowly, so changes inside individual companies can't be followed from the outside.
